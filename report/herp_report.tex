\begin{abstract}
\noindent
This report summarises the experiment design and current empirical status of the
Minimum Viable Product (MVP) for \emph{receiver-aware experience routing}. A
population of independently learning SAC policies discovers complementary
functional experiences; a router transports those experiences from strong
\emph{donors} to weak \emph{receivers}. The MVP runs the full pipeline
end-to-end on a fast synthetic pick-and-place environment (CPU). The heavy RL
backbone and the Meta-World suite are wired as drop-in swaps for a later GPU
session. The central question the MVP answers is \emph{whether the simple
state-transition pipeline produces a non-trivial routing signal} --- and it
does: complementary competence emerges (Gate~C passes) and the unbalanced
optimal-transport (UOT) router transports experience over the discovered valid
set. The headline numbers below are from the synthetic environment; the
routing-comparison magnitudes on a real suite are pending a GPU run.
\end{abstract}

%--------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Scientific claim under test}
The MVP is designed to test one narrow, falsifiable claim (PLAN.md \S30):
\begin{quote}\itshape
A population can be organised into a dynamic functional-experience competence
space; the resulting receiver deficits can be used to route experience from
policies that have already mastered a functional transition to policies that
have not, outperforming indiscriminate or globally prioritised experience
sharing.
\end{quote}
The corresponding ablation hierarchy is
\emph{Independent} $\rightarrow$ \emph{Shared / global-priority} $\rightarrow$
\emph{Receiver deficit + greedy donor} $\rightarrow$ \emph{Receiver deficit +
UOT}.

The pipeline (PLAN.md \S15) is:
\[
\text{Parallel SAC}\to\text{segmentation}\to\text{experience grouping}\to
\text{validity filter}\to\text{competence}\to\text{deficit}\to
\text{router}\to\text{routed replay}\to\text{updates.}
\]

\noindent\textit{Reading of Gates D--E.} PLAN.md \S25 states explicitly that if
greedy is as good as UOT, OT should be dropped rather than forced into the
paper. On the current synthetic environment the routing signal is real but the
\emph{magnitude} of the greedy-over-baseline and UOT-over-greedy gaps is small.
This is consistent with a small, easy environment (few valid experiences, modest
donor competence): greedy $\operatorname{argmax}$ nearly coincides with the OT
solution when the complementarity structure is simple. Establishing decisive
Gate~D/E magnitudes is the primary reason to run on Meta-World (harder task,
larger deficits and higher-quality supply).

\section{Summary}
\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
  \item The full MVP pipeline runs end-to-end on the synthetic environment and
        is unit-tested, including the OT gate (Table~\ref{tab:tests}).
  \item Complementary competence emerges --- Gate~C passes, with three of four
        policies each leading on some experience (Table~\ref{tab:complementarity}).
  \item The UOT router produces a real, non-trivial routing signal (117
        transported chunks; sub-millisecond solve, Table~\ref{tab:headline}).
  \item Gate~D/E magnitudes are positive but small on the synthetic
        environment; deciding whether OT earns its place over greedy requires a
        harder suite (Meta-World, GPU), for which the backbone is already
        wired as a drop-in swap.
\end{itemize}
